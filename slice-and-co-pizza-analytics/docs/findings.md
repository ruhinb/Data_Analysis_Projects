For: the General Manager. Source: 48,620 order lines, 1 January to 31 December 2015. Figures match the report with no slicers applied.

Two rush windows (12:00–1:30 PM and 4:30–8:00 PM) bring in 56% of revenue. Sales were flat across the year: monthly revenue stayed between \$64.0K (October) and \$72.6K (July). The second half was about 2% softer than the first, driven by order count rather than spend per order.

## 1. Demand peaks twice a day, and Friday is the busiest day

- **Lunch is the sharpest peak**: about 7 orders per trading day at 12 PM and 1 PM, against 3–4 in the hours either side. Lunch demand is concentrated in roughly 12 PM–1:30 PM.
- **Dinner is longer and flatter**: 5:00–7:00 PM runs at 6.5–6.7 orders per hour, with elevated demand from about 4:30 PM to 8 PM.
- **Friday averages 70.8 orders per trading day**, Sunday 50.5 (the quietest). Thursday (62.3) and Saturday (60.7) follow Friday.
- Orders arrive on average **every 11.0 minutes** during trading hours.

**Recommendation:** finish dough and topping prep before 11:30 AM and 4:30 PM; schedule the strongest kitchen team on Friday lunch and Friday 5–7 PM; Sunday is the natural day for training, deep cleaning and lighter rosters.

## 2. Menu: chicken pizzas earn the most, classics sell the most

| Category | Revenue | Share | Pizzas sold | Share | Avg price |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Classic | $220,053 | 26.9% | 14,888 | 30.0% | $14.78 |
| Supreme | $208,197 | 25.5% | 11,987 | 24.2% | $17.37 |
| Chicken | $195,920 | 24.0% | 11,050 | 22.3% | $17.73 |
| Veggie | $193,690 | 23.7% | 11,649 | 23.5% | $16.63 |

Classic ranks first on both revenue and quantity; Chicken ranks third on revenue but fourth on quantity because it carries the highest average price.

- **Top 5 by revenue:** Thai Chicken (\$43,434), Barbecue Chicken (\$42,768), California Chicken (\$41,410), Classic Deluxe (\$38,181), Spicy Italian (\$34,831). The three chicken pizzas alone bring in 15.6% of revenue.
- **Top 5 by quantity:** Classic Deluxe (2,453), Barbecue Chicken (2,432), Hawaiian (2,422), Pepperoni (2,418), Thai Chicken (2,371).
- **Bottom 5 by revenue:** Brie Carre (\$11,589), Green Garden (\$13,956), Spinach Supreme (\$15,278), Mediterranean (\$15,361), Spinach Pesto (\$15,596).
- **Bottom 5 by quantity:** Brie Carre (490), Mediterranean (934), Calabrese (937), Spinach Supreme (950), Soppressata (961).
- **Sizes:** Large 38.2% of pizzas, Medium 31.5%, Small 29.1%; XL (1.1%) and XXL (0.1%) exist only for The Greek. Classic is the only category where Small is the best seller; Chicken, Supreme and Veggie customers buy mostly Large.
- **Price vs volume:** across the 32 pizzas the correlation between average price and pizzas sold is weak and negative (r = −0.31). Across the four categories it looks strong (r = −0.95) but rests on four points; the cheapest category (Classic) sells the most.
- **Basket:** 38.0% of orders (8,111) contain a single pizza. They average \$16.52, against \$51.66 for multi-pizza orders. If one in ten of them added a second pizza at the average price (\$16.50), revenue would rise by about \$13,400 a year (+1.6%).

**Most and least popular ingredients by category** (pizzas sold containing the ingredient):

| Category | Most popular | Least popular |
|---|---|---|
| Classic | Pepperoni (8,144) | Green Peppers (1,359) |
| Supreme | Garlic (10,530) | Brie Carre Cheese (490) |
| Chicken | Red Peppers (8,680) | Pesto Sauce (973) |
| Veggie | Garlic (9,718) | Kalamata Olives (934) |

Garlic appears in the most pizzas overall (27,913). Tomatoes, red onions and red peppers follow; these drive purchasing volumes.

**Recommendations:**
1. Feature the chicken trio and Classic Deluxe in bundles and online placement; they combine volume and price.
2. Review Brie Carre: it is sold only as a Small, priced at \$23.65 (more than any Large on the menu, the dearest of which is \$21.00), and sold 490 units. Reprice it, offer it in larger sizes, or replace it.
3. Test a second-pizza or sides offer aimed at the 38% of single-pizza orders.
4. Keep XL/XXL under review; together they are about 1% of volume and only one pizza offers them.

## 3. Unusual trading days have clear causes

Each trading day is compared with the other days of the same weekday (leave-one-out mean and standard deviation). A day is flagged when revenue, or failing that order count, is at least 2 standard deviations from that baseline. 21 days were flagged.

| Date | Flag | Revenue vs typical same weekday | Likely context |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Sat 4 Jul | High revenue | +65% (z = 6.4) | Independence Day |
| Fri 27 Nov | High revenue | +65% | Black Friday |
| Sun 1 Feb | High revenue | +69% | Super Bowl Sunday |
| Thu 26 Nov | High revenue | +89% | Thanksgiving |
| Thu 15 Oct | High revenue | +85% | No calendar event; check promotions or local events |
| Sat 26 – Wed 30 Dec | Low revenue / low orders | down to −40% (30 Dec) | Week after Christmas |

On 4 July the extra revenue came from more orders (+75%), not bigger baskets (−6%), and the busiest hour was 9–10 PM (fireworks evening), three times a typical Saturday at that hour.

The nine high-revenue days brought in about \$11,400 more than typical same-weekday trading; the eight low days cost about \$5,400.

**Recommendation:** build a trading calendar with Independence Day, Super Bowl Sunday, Thanksgiving week and Black Friday: extra dough, extra evening cover, and pre-planned promotions. Plan reduced hours and lean rosters between Christmas and New Year. Investigate 15 October with the store team.

## 4. Workforce: the baseline roster is heavy in the afternoon

| Measure | Baseline roster | Demand-based recommendation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Labour cost | $310,525 | $270,896 |
| Labour cost as % of revenue | 38.0% | 33.1% |
| Potential saving | | $39,629 (12.8%) |

- The baseline carries **4–5 more people than demand needs between about 3 PM and 6 PM**, where overlapping shifts meet the afternoon lull. That window holds 36% of scheduled hours but only 27% of orders.
- **Sunday** offers the largest saving (\$12.5K), followed by Saturday (\$10.2K) and Friday (\$7.9K): the weekend template is applied to Sunday although Sunday is the quietest day.
- At lunch the baseline is close to the recommendation; demand there is covered.

**Recommendation :** stagger afternoon shift starts so fewer people overlap from 3 PM; give Sunday its own lighter template; pilot for four weeks while tracking order times and guest feedback before rolling out.

## Method notes

- Comparisons: one month selected → previous month; whole year (no earlier data) → second half vs first half.
- Recommended roster rule: 1 manager; cooks = ceil(pizzas × 1.15 ÷ 5) per half-hour; service crew = ceil(orders × 1.15 ÷ 4) per half-hour; minimum one of each; one prep person 9:30 AM–5:30 PM.
